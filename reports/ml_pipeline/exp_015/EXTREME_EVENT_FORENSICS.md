# EXP-015 extreme-event forensics

`EXTREME_EVENT_ROWS.csv` lists ten scored rows, but they represent only **five unique Train station-hours**, each reused with a public and private cutoff. The two Borivali East records on 2025-08-09 are 75,203.525 and 25,007.455 µg/m³. The three Byculla records on 2026-05-09 are 1,762.91, 3,320.6333, and 1,643.1375. These are unique `(location_id, target_start)` records in Train, not duplicate CSV rows. Both Borivali records are from `openaq:12236418`; the Byculla records from `openaq:12238567`. The `flag_high_1000` field is true on all five, with count 3–4 constituent measurements. The flag documents exceptional magnitude, **not** confirmed invalidity. No independent instrument log or organizer correction notice was found, so physical plausibility and fault status are unresolved.

The five observations account for **99.9862%** of public and **99.9797%** of private W50 squared error on the EXP-014 rows. The same events were inspected in EXP-013 and EXP-014; recounting them across experiments or two issue states does not create new evidence. The public/private predictions at these targets differ with cutoff, but both remain tens of µg/m³ while labels reach thousands to tens of thousands. A seasonal-mean correction of a few units cannot materially address this tail. This explains why candidate full RMSE differences of hundredths coexist with meaningful ordinary-range changes.

For diagnosis only, `y < 500` RMSE is 22.547 public and 27.342 private for W50, versus full 1913.800 and 1918.166. The original labels are retained in every primary metric and all candidate comparisons. The extreme events are not relabelled, excluded, or declared corrupt. Rows near these events can be correlated through the 48-hour fold window, and the exact same five labels enter both issue regimes. No candidate is recommended from a tiny full-RMSE improvement that depends solely on these events.
