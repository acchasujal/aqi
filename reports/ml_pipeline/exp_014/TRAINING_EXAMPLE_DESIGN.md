# EXP-014 supervised example design

One training example is `(station, prediction_origin, PM cutoff, target interval, eligible PM and calendar features) -> future PM2.5`. `build_examples` first builds all predictor rows from explicit historical origins and only then joins the original Train label on `(location_id, target_start)`. `target_end > prediction_origin` is enforced. All PM-derived inputs use station observations whose hourly **interval end** is no later than the origin's PM cutoff; `target_start` is the interval start. Missing elapsed hours remain absent rather than zero or arbitrary forward fills. The 2024 archive predates every 2025–26 origin. Raw `count`, `available_at`, IDs, flags and future weather are excluded.

Historical target days start 16 days after the first Train hour and advance every seven days. Each target day contributes a 48-hour window in two separate regimes, where labels exist:

| Regime | First target minus PM cutoff | Issue time relative to PM cutoff | Eligible actual station lead |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Public-shaped | 1 hour | At cutoff | 1–48 hours |
| Private-shaped | 193 hours | 109 hours after cutoff | 193–240 hours |

The private 109-hour offset reproduces the audited November 2 PM cutoff to November 7 12:00 IST run time; the first private target is 84 hours after that issue. A station-specific last-reading outage increases actual lead, and examples outside the regime's actual-lead band are excluded before the label join. `EXAMPLE_COUNTS.csv` gives counts by regime, station and issue month. The final source snapshot produced **26,580** examples. The exact feature schema and input hashes are recorded in `RUN_METADATA.json`.

Validation uses fixed, sparse historical target days, including two previously known extreme periods as stress checks. Each validation fold fits only examples with `target_end <= validation PM cutoff`; this purges every later label, including examples of the opposite regime. Public and private folds for a target day have different cutoffs and are fit separately. No validation target is fed back as an input or used to fit its own model. The 22 scored folds contain **1,719 public** and **1,711 private** target rows per model, across eleven target days; four stations appear in every fold, but station-hours are incomplete. Every candidate and W50 uses identical scoring keys and raw labels. Historical `available_at` is not a verified first-publication time, so the PM-cutoff release simulation remains an explicit proxy assumption.

The same target label may appear once in each regime with different cutoff-safe feature states. These paired examples and paired validation rows are not independent observations, so public and private metrics are reported separately. Folds are development and stress evidence. Dates and many individual rows overlap prior EXP-010–013 exploration, and the two extreme periods were already identified. No fold is described as an untouched independent confirmation set. Regime-specific and fold-wise counts are in `FOLD_COVERAGE.csv`; the ignored row-level cache can be regenerated by `scripts/validate_supervised.py`.
